06 · BUYER'S CHECKLIST

What to check before you buy.

Ten minutes with the car tells you more than any ad. Bring a flashlight and a paper towel - here is where to point them.

750 RPM · STEADY
Step 01 COLD ENGINE TEST

Cold start & idle

Insist the hood stays cold before you arrive. RPMs should flare once, then settle smoothly near 750 rpm. In Canadian winters, cold starts expose weak batteries and noisy timing tensioners.

Red flag: metallic rattle or hunting idle that persists past 30 seconds.
ACTIVE DRIP · FLAG
Step 02 DRY PAN = PASS

Oil & pan leaks

Sweep a bright flashlight under the front subframe, valve covers, and driveway spot. Road grime is normal on daily drivers — glistening fresh oil beads mean active gasket seepage.

Red flag: wet drops hanging from the sump plug or fresh puddles on pavement.
BLUE · OIL BURN
Step 03 WARM IDLE CHECK

Exhaust smoke

Brief white condensation at minus-10°C clears in two minutes. Persistent blue haze means worn rings/seals, thick sweet white clouds mean head gasket coolant burn, black means over-fueling.

Red flag: visible coloured smoke after the temperature gauge reaches normal.
PARK · LOCKED
Step 04 SHIFT < 1.0S

Transmission feel

Foot firmly on the brake, cycle P-R-N-D slowly. Engagement should take under a second with zero metallic clunk. On highway ramps, watch for torque-converter shudder at 60–100 km/h.

Red flag: 2-second hesitation followed by a hard jolt into Reverse or Drive.
SALT BELT ZONES
Step 05 SALT BELT CHECK

Rust & salt-belt zones

Inspect rear wheel arches, rocker pinch welds, lower door seams, and the spare-tire well. In Ontario, Quebec, and Atlantic Canada, road salt hides deep perforation beneath fresh paint.

Red flag: bubbling clearcoat along arches or soft, flaky rocker pinch seams.
AMBER · CLEAN OIL
Step 06 HEAD GASKET CHECK

Oil & coolant condition

Honey-amber oil on a clean towel is healthy. Thick black sludge points to skipped changes; chocolate-milk froth on the dipstick or oil cap means coolant is mixing inside the engine.

Red flag: milky tan paste on the dipstick — walk away immediately.

09 · QUESTIONS

Before you ask.

Is the VIN check really free?
Yes. The free check decodes any 17-character VIN and shows the vehicle's make, model, trim, engine, open safety recalls, an estimated market value and the make's known weak points. No sign-up, no card.
What does the full Drivee report include?
The full Drivee Canadian report ($17 CAD single, $39 CAD 3-pack, or $99 CAD 10-pack) unlocks instant live generation in your browser plus downloadable PDF: accident and damage history, odometer readings in km with a rollback check, previous owners and use type, theft/branding records and Transport Canada recalls.
How do I check for a lien on a used car in Canada?
Drivee retired the $30 Ontario lien add-on so single reports could drop to $17 CAD and multi-car packs save up to 42%. For a registered lien check before buying, run a ServiceOntario UVIP (~$20) in Ontario or your province's PPSA registry, paired with a Drivee vehicle history dossier.
Can a VIN check detect odometer rollback?
The full report plots every recorded odometer reading over time. If a later reading is lower than an earlier one, that is a rollback flag. The sample report on this page shows what a clean, consistent mileage graph looks like.
Is Drivee affiliated with CARFAX?
No. Drivee is an independent Toronto-built service. Full reports are compiled from industry vehicle-history data and government sources, then delivered in a clear, plain-English format.
What should I check in person before buying a used car?
Cold start and steady idle near 750 rpm, fresh oil leaks, exhaust smoke colour, transmission engagement under one second, rust on arches and rockers, and fluid condition - amber oil is fine, milky oil means walk away. See the illustrated checklist above.
