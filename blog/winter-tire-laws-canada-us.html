Winter tire laws in Canada and the US: where they're required, and where they're not

Short answer

In Canada, only Quebec and British Columbia require winter tires, and BC only on signed highways. Ontario doesn't require them, but insurers must offer a discount if you use them. In the US, the rules you're most likely to meet are traction and chain laws on mountain roads, which apply by road and by storm rather than by date.

Drivee Editorial Updated November 15, 2026 9 min read

Winter tire laws in Canada and the US are a patchwork. One province fines you on a fixed date, another only on posted highways, and several US states care less about your tires than about whether chains are in the trunk. If you drive across provinces or the border this winter, here's what applies where, with the dates and fines from the official sources.

Are winter tires mandatory in Canada? Winter tire laws at a glance

Not nationally. There is no federal winter tire law in Canada. The Tire and Rubber Association of Canada (TRAC) notes that only Quebec and British Columbia have winter tire mandates. Everywhere else, winter tires are a choice, though a choice your insurer, your stopping distance and some park and municipal roads may push you toward.

Quebec winter tire law: December 1 to March 15

Quebec has the clearest rule in Canada. From December 1 to March 15, every motor vehicle registered in Quebec, including rental cars, must be on winter tires. Heavy vehicles, tool vehicles and farm machinery are excluded. The fine for not complying is $200 to $300, according to the Quebec government.

Two details catch people:

  • Only the right symbol counts. Since December 15, 2014, a tire is a winter tire under Quebec's Highway Safety Code only if it carries the mountain-and-snowflake pictogram or is studded. An all-season tire marked only M+S does not qualify.
  • Out-of-province cars are not covered. The requirement does not apply to vehicles registered outside Quebec, so an Ontario or US plate driving to Montreal isn't breaking the law on all-seasons. You'd still be driving Quebec roads in Quebec weather.

SAAQ also recommends at least 4.8 mm of tread across the full width of the tire when you install it.

BC winter tire rules: signed highways, October 1 to April 30

British Columbia ties its rule to the road, not the whole province. On designated highways with winter tire signs, passenger vehicles need proper tires from October 1 to April 30. On select highways that aren't mountain passes or high-snowfall areas, the requirement ends on March 31. Many coastal areas, including much of the Lower Mainland, don't require them, though some routes such as the Malahat do.

What counts in BC:

  • tires marked M+S or with the three-peak mountain snowflake,
  • at least 3.5 mm of tread depth.

The province is explicit that chains on summer tires are not a substitute for legal winter tires on signed BC highways. Drivers who don't comply may be turned away and fined. Check DriveBC before mountain trips, because conditions and closures change by the hour.

Are winter tires mandatory in Ontario?

No. Ontario has no winter tire requirement for passenger vehicles. What Ontario does have is an insurance rule: for private passenger auto policies issued or renewed on or after January 1, 2016, insurers must offer a discount for vehicles equipped with winter tires. The Financial Services Regulatory Authority of Ontario (FSRA) says all insurance companies must offer it and tells drivers to ask their insurer how to qualify.

There is no fixed percentage in the rule. Each insurer sets its own discount and conditions, such as having four winter tires on for a set part of the season, so call your insurer or broker and ask directly. If you already have winter tires and aren't getting the discount, that's money left on the table.

Ontario's government also says winter tires can shorten braking distances by as much as 25%, and advises installing four of them, never mixing tire types on one vehicle.

Are winter tires required in the US?

Rather than a calendar rule like Quebec's, US states mostly regulate traction by road and by storm. Two of the best-known examples:

Colorado. The Traction Law applies on I-70 between Morrison and Dotsero from September 1 to May 31, and elsewhere when activated. Passenger vehicles need tires with at least 3/16 inch of tread that are all-weather or winter tires marked with the mountain snowflake or M+S, or chains or an approved alternative traction device. In severe storms CDOT can switch on the Passenger Vehicle Chain Law, the last step before closing a highway, when every vehicle needs chains or an approved device. Fines per CDOT: $100 plus a $33 surcharge, or $500 plus a $157 surcharge if your vehicle blocks a travel lane.

California. Caltrans posts chain controls at three levels:

  • R-1: chains required except on passenger vehicles and light trucks under 6,000 pounds with snow tires on at least two drive wheels. Those vehicles must still carry chains.
  • R-2: chains or traction devices required on all vehicles except four-wheel or all-wheel drive vehicles with snow-tread tires on all four wheels, which must still carry them.
  • R-3: chains or traction devices required on every vehicle, no exceptions.

Tip: if you're renting a car for a ski trip in the western US, ask whether chains are allowed on it before you need them.

Where are studded tires legal?

Studs are where the rules diverge most. Some places allow them for a season, some limit who can use them, and some ban them for residents altogether.

Ontario's rule comes from Regulation 625 under the Highway Traffic Act, which defines Northern Ontario as the districts of Algoma, Cochrane, Kenora, Manitoulin, Nipissing, Parry Sound, Rainy River, Sudbury, Timiskaming and Thunder Bay. A Toronto or Ottawa driver can't legally run studs, even on a trip north.

What's the difference between M+S and the mountain snowflake symbol?

The three-peak mountain snowflake (sometimes called 3PMSF or the Alpine symbol) means the manufacturer certifies the tire passed a standardized snow traction test. Transport Canada describes it as identifying tires that give a higher level of snow traction suited to winter driving.

M+S (mud and snow) has no such test behind it. Transport Canada says M+S, or all-season, tires give safe all-weather performance but may not be suitable for severe snow. That's why Quebec doesn't accept M+S alone, while BC and Colorado do.

You'll also see all-weather tires that carry the mountain snowflake. They are legal winter tires in Quebec and can be a reasonable middle ground for city drivers, though a dedicated winter tire is built for colder, icier conditions.

When should you put winter tires on?

TRAC's rule of thumb is to switch once the temperature consistently drops below 7°C, because all-season rubber starts to harden and lose grip around that point while winter compounds stay soft. In southern Ontario that point tends to arrive in late fall. Don't wait for the first snowfall, when every tire shop is booked solid.

A few habits that keep winter tires working:

  • Install four, never two. TRAC warns that winter tires on one axle only can cause oversteer or understeer.
  • Check pressure monthly. Pressure drops as it gets colder.
  • Watch tread depth. Transport Canada advises keeping winter tires with at least 50% of their tread left.
  • Store them right. Clean, out of the sun, away from heat sources, and stacked or hung as the manufacturer recommends.

Common questions

Are winter tires mandatory in Canada?

Not nationally. Quebec requires winter tires on Quebec-registered vehicles from December 1 to March 15, and British Columbia requires winter tires on signed designated highways, generally from October 1 to April 30. Other provinces, including Ontario, do not require them.

Are winter tires mandatory in Ontario?

No. Ontario does not require winter tires on passenger vehicles. Since January 1, 2016, insurers have had to offer a discount on private passenger auto policies for vehicles with winter tires. The amount and conditions vary by insurer, so ask yours how to qualify.

Do I need winter tires to drive in Quebec with an Ontario or US plate?

No. Quebec's winter tire requirement applies to vehicles registered in Quebec, including rentals, not to vehicles registered outside the province. Winter tires are still the safer choice for Quebec winter roads.

Do all-season M+S tires count as winter tires?

It depends where you are. Quebec only accepts tires with the three-peak mountain snowflake symbol or studded tires. British Columbia's signed highways and Colorado's traction law accept M+S tires with enough tread: 3.5 mm in BC and 3/16 inch in Colorado.

Are studded tires legal in Ontario?

Only for some drivers. Ontario's Regulation 625 allows studded tires from September 1 to May 31 if the vehicle's permit holder is a Northern Ontario resident or one of certain Northern Ontario public bodies, or a non-resident whose vehicle is in Ontario for no more than 30 days. Drivers from southern Ontario cannot use them.

When should I put winter tires on?

The Tire and Rubber Association of Canada recommends switching once temperatures consistently fall below 7°C, when all-season tires start to harden and lose grip. In Quebec, they must be on by December 1.
