Distracted driving fines by province and state: texting and driving penalties in Canada and the US

Holding the phone is enough

In most provinces you don't have to be texting to be charged. Simply holding a phone while driving, even stopped at a red light, can cost you a fine, demerit points and in some provinces an immediate suspension.

Drivee Editorial Updated November 15, 2026 9 min read

Distracted driving fines by province and state vary more than most drivers expect. A handheld phone ticket costs $390 in Alberta, $615 in Ontario and $672 in Manitoba, and a repeat offence can mean a suspension or a seized car. Here is what each province charges, how the US compares, and what to do if you get one.

Ontario distracted driving fines and suspensions

Ontario's penalties are among the steepest in the country once you count the court maximums and the suspensions. For drivers with A to G licences:

  • First conviction: $615 out of court, up to $1,000 if summoned or convicted in court, 3 demerit points and a 3-day suspension.
  • Second conviction: $615 out of court, up to $2,000 in court, 6 points and a 7-day suspension.
  • Third and further convictions: $615 out of court, up to $3,000 in court, 6 points and a 30-day suspension.

The $615 already includes the victim surcharge and court fee. You also pay a licence reinstatement fee every time your licence is suspended.

If police believe your distraction put others at risk, they can lay careless driving instead, whether the distraction was a handheld phone, a hands-free device or something else. A careless driving conviction carries 6 demerit points, fines up to $2,000, possible jail of up to six months and a suspension of up to two years.

Which US states ban handheld phones while driving?

According to the Governors Highway Safety Association's latest summary, last updated in October 2025:

  • 33 states ban handheld cellphone use for all drivers. Washington DC, Puerto Rico, Guam, the Northern Mariana Islands and the US Virgin Islands do too.
  • 49 states ban texting for all drivers.
  • 36 states and DC ban all cellphone use, including hands-free, by novice drivers.

Fines are much lower than in Canada, but points are common. Three examples:

StateFinePoints
New York$50 to $200 first offence, up to $250 (2nd) and $450 (3rd) within 18 months, plus a surcharge5

The base fine is rarely what you pay in the US. California's Office of Traffic Safety put the real cost of a first ticket at $160 or more once assessments are added.

What counts as distracted driving? Phones at red lights, GPS and more

The details differ by province, but the core rule is the same: your hands stay off the device while you are in control of the car.

Stopped at a red light still counts

Ontario's law applies while you are stopped in traffic or at a red light. Alberta's law applies even when you are stopped at a red light. Quebec considers you to be driving at a red light, and CAA-Quebec notes that plugging a phone in to charge or taking it out of its mount there is prohibited too. Pull over and park safely before you pick it up.

Mounted phones and GPS

  • Ontario: a securely mounted phone or GPS is allowed, but you may only touch it to make, answer or end a call. Programming a GPS by hand is not allowed; use voice.
  • Alberta: a GPS is allowed if it is mounted and set up before you drive.
  • British Columbia: one touch to start, accept or end a call, with the device securely mounted and the route programmed before you set off.
  • Quebec: the device must sit in a mount designed for it and be used hands-free or by voice.

Other screens and exceptions

Ontario also bans viewing a display screen unrelated to driving, such as a video. Calling 911 is allowed in Ontario, Alberta, British Columbia and Newfoundland and Labrador, though Ontario recommends pulling off the road first. Eating, drinking or grooming are not part of Ontario's handheld law, but Alberta's distracted driving law does cover reading, writing and personal grooming.

What happens to new drivers?

New drivers face harsher rules, and often the penalty that matters is the suspension rather than the fine.

  • Ontario (G1, G2, M1, M2): the same fines, no demerit points, but a 30-day suspension on a first conviction, 90 days on a second, and cancellation of the licence on a third, which means starting graduated licensing again.
  • British Columbia (L and N): no electronic device use at all, hands-free and GPS included. The fine is the same $368 and 4 points, and a driving prohibition restarts the 24-month Novice period.
  • New York (junior and probationary): a 120-day suspension on a first conviction.

Does a distracted driving ticket raise insurance?

It can, and for many drivers the insurance cost outlasts the fine. A distracted driving ticket is a conviction on your driving record, and Ontario's insurance regulator lists convictions among the factors insurers use to set your rate.

Some provinces add their own costs on top:

  • British Columbia: two or more distracted driving tickets in three years bring a Driver Risk Premium. The province says total financial penalties can reach $2,000, on top of regular premiums.
  • Saskatchewan: demerits can cost you earned discounts, and a driver below zero on the Safe Driver Recognition scale pays $50 for every point below zero.
  • Manitoba: the 5 demerits move you down the Driver Safety Rating scale, which affects what you pay.

How much a single ticket costs you elsewhere depends on your insurer and your record. If you are near renewal, ask your broker how a conviction would be treated before you decide what to do with the ticket.

What to do after a distracted driving ticket

In Ontario, you have 15 days to choose an option on the back of the ticket. If you don't respond, you can be convicted without a hearing.

  1. Pay the fine. This is a guilty plea. You get the conviction, the points and the suspension.
  2. Early resolution, where your court offers it. You meet a prosecutor before any trial. Sometimes a charge is resolved on different terms; nothing is guaranteed.
  3. Request a trial. The prosecution must prove you were holding or using the device. You can ask for disclosure, including the officer's notes, before the trial date.

Licensed paralegals in Ontario can represent drivers on Highway Traffic Act charges. A paralegal is worth a conversation if you hold a G1 or G2 and face a suspension, if this is a second or third conviction, if the new points would bring you close to a suspension, or if you drive for a living. Many offer a free consultation, so ask what outcome is realistic and what it will cost.

Outside Ontario, the steps and deadlines are printed on the ticket, and they differ. Read them the day you get it.

Common questions

How much is a distracted driving ticket in Ontario?

$615 if you pay the ticket out of court, which includes the victim surcharge and court fee. If you go to court or are summoned, a first conviction can be up to $1,000, a second up to $2,000 and a third or later up to $3,000. Fully licensed drivers also get 3 demerit points and a 3-day suspension on a first conviction.

Which province has the highest distracted driving fine?

It depends on how you count. Manitoba's $672 is the highest fixed first-offence fine in our table, Prince Edward Island's range runs up to $1,275, and Ontario allows up to $3,000 in court for a third or later conviction. Saskatchewan escalates fastest, reaching $2,100 and a vehicle seizure on a third ticket within a year.

Can you hold your phone at a red light?

Not in Ontario, Alberta or Quebec. Ontario's law applies while you are stopped in traffic or at a red light, Alberta's applies even when you are stopped at a red light, and Quebec treats you as still driving at a red light. Pull over and park safely first.

Is it legal to use GPS on a mounted phone?

Generally yes, if the phone is securely mounted and you set the route before you drive or by voice. Ontario does not allow programming a GPS on a hand-held device except by voice. In British Columbia, drivers in the Graduated Licensing Program may not use any electronic device, including GPS.

How many US states ban handheld phone use while driving?

33 states ban handheld cellphone use for all drivers, according to the Governors Highway Safety Association, and 49 states ban texting for all drivers. Washington DC and several territories also ban handheld use.

Does a distracted driving ticket raise insurance?

It can. A distracted driving ticket is a conviction on your driving record, and insurers use convictions to set rates. In British Columbia, two or more distracted driving tickets in three years also bring a Driver Risk Premium, and in Saskatchewan demerits can cost you discounts or add financial penalties.
