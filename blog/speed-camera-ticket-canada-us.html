Speed camera tickets: do they add points or raise insurance? (Canada and US)

The deadline still counts

No points does not mean no consequences. Most camera tickets give you 30 to 45 days to pay or dispute, and an unpaid one can block your plate or licence renewal.

Drivee Editorial Updated November 15, 2026 8 min read

A speed camera ticket arrives in the mail, addressed to whoever owns the car, often weeks after the drive. The first question most people ask is whether it will cost them demerit points or push up their insurance. In most of Canada and the US the answer is no, but there are exceptions, and the deadline on the notice matters as much as the fine.

How does a speed camera ticket work?

A camera records a vehicle going over the limit and captures the licence plate. It cannot reliably identify the person behind the wheel, so the ticket is sent to the registered owner of the plate. That single fact explains almost everything else about camera tickets.

  • The owner is liable whoever was driving. Lending your car to a friend does not move the ticket to them.
  • It is usually not a driver conviction. Because no driver is identified, most systems keep the ticket off the driving record.
  • The fine is the main cost. In most places there are no points and no licence suspension tied to the ticket itself.

Some places have closed the old loophole of naming the driver. British Columbia's intersection camera program has made the owner responsible since September 1, 2018, and Quebec stopped letting owners identify another driver for photo radar tickets as of July 1, 2024.

A ticket from a police officer who pulls you over is different. That is a charge against you as the driver, with demerit points and a conviction on your record. If that is what you have, see our guide to speeding tickets and demerit points in Ontario.

One caution for US drivers: "no points" is the norm, not a rule. The Insurance Institute for Highway Safety's state table lists Arizona (up to a $250 fine and 3 points) and Michigan (1 to 5 points depending on speed) as states where a speed camera ticket can carry points. Commercial vehicles are another exception: in British Columbia, a conviction on a commercial vehicle adds points to the owner's National Safety Code profile.

Will a speed camera ticket raise my insurance?

Usually not, and for a simple reason. Insurers set rates using your driving record, and Ontario's insurance regulator lists tickets and convictions among the factors they use. A camera ticket that never reaches your record gives them nothing to rate.

Several programs say this in plain terms:

  • Toronto says its camera penalties will not affect your driving record or demerit points.
  • Calgary Police say automated tickets carry no demerit points and do not affect your driving record or insurance.
  • New York City says camera violations are not part of your DMV operating record and are not used for insurance purposes.
  • Maryland's speed camera law says a camera violation is not a moving violation for points and may not be considered for insurance coverage.

The exception is a camera ticket that does carry points, as in Arizona or Michigan. Points mean a record entry, and a record entry can be rated. If you are unsure, call your broker or agent before you pay and ask how your insurer treats it.

Are there still speed cameras in Ontario?

No. As of November 2026, municipal speed cameras are banned across Ontario.

  • The law: Bill 56, the Building a More Competitive Economy Act, 2025, repealed the part of the Highway Traffic Act that allowed automated speed enforcement. The change took effect on November 14, 2025. The bill passed the legislature in late October 2025 after Premier Doug Ford called the cameras a cash grab.
  • Older tickets still count: the City of Toronto says penalty orders for violations before November 14, 2025 remain valid and must be paid or disputed within 30 days of issuance.
  • Which lookup to use: Toronto violations on or before January 19, 2025 are court matters (Court Case Lookup). Violations from January 20, 2025 to November 13, 2025 went through the city's Administrative Penalty System (Administrative Penalty Lookup).
  • Red light cameras were not affected. They still operate, and work much the same way. See our guide to red light camera tickets in Toronto.

Toronto's camera penalties were scaled by how far over the limit the vehicle was. At 50 km/h or more over, there was no set amount and the owner was summoned to court instead. We found no legislation to bring speed cameras back as of this update. Police speed enforcement with radar and laser continues as before, and those tickets do carry points.

Alberta has narrowed photo radar too

Since April 1, 2025, Alberta's provincial guideline bars photo radar on provincial highways and connectors, except in school, playground and construction zones. Intersection cameras there now enforce red lights only. Cities such as Calgary still run photo radar on local roads.

How do you dispute a speed camera ticket?

The process depends on who issued it, but the pattern is similar everywhere: a short deadline, a review or court date, and a decision based on the evidence in the photos.

Toronto (administrative penalties)

  1. Screening review. Request it online through the Administrative Penalty Lookup within 30 days of the deemed service date. You get one request per penalty order, so upload everything at once. The two grounds are that the violation did not occur, or that paying would cause undue hardship (which needs your Canada Revenue Agency Notice of Assessment). Toronto lists a turnaround of up to 60 days.
  2. Hearing review. If you disagree with the screening decision, ask for a hearing within 30 days of that decision. The hearing officer's decision is final.

If you miss the first 30 days, you can ask for an extension of up to 60 days from the deemed service date, together with your screening request. Missing a scheduled screening or hearing adds a $60 non-appearance fee. Any payment, even a partial one, ends your right to dispute. The steps mirror disputing a Toronto parking ticket.

Other provinces and states

  • British Columbia: file a Notice of Dispute by mail or at a court registry or ICBC driver licensing office. It must be postmarked within 45 days of mailing, or 30 days if the ticket was served in person. A court date follows.
  • Alberta: use the options on the ticket, online at traffictickets.alberta.ca or by mail, by the date printed on it.
  • Quebec: enter a plea of not guilty within 30 days of receiving the statement of offence.
  • New York City: respond to the Notice of Liability within 30 days. An owner whose car was stolen can submit the police report.

Tip: "I wasn't driving" rarely works, because the owner is liable by design. Arguments that do work look at the evidence: a misread plate, a different vehicle in the photo, a stolen car, or a ticket that arrived outside the legal time limit.

What happens if you don't pay a speed camera ticket?

Without points, an unpaid camera ticket is a debt, and governments collect debts through the things you need to renew.

  • Toronto: a $20 late payment fee applies if the penalty is unpaid 60 days after it becomes due, then collection procedures and possible licence plate denial.
  • British Columbia: you must pay the ticket before you can renew your driver's licence or car insurance, unless you are disputing it.
  • Maryland: if the penalty is neither paid nor contested, the Motor Vehicle Administration may refuse to register the vehicle or suspend its registration.
  • New York City: late penalties are added, and owners with 15 or more school speed camera violations in 12 months may have to complete a safe vehicle operation course, with vehicle seizure possible if they don't.

What about an out-of-province or out-of-state camera ticket?

Camera programs find owners through plate registration records, and that works across borders more often than people expect. Ontario's regulation, for example, includes a fee on every camera penalty to cover looking up the owner's name and address.

What changes is enforcement. A home-province tool like plate denial or a registration hold works most directly on vehicles registered in that jurisdiction, so an out-of-province ticket is more likely to be chased through collections. Because a camera ticket is an owner penalty rather than a driver conviction, it generally has no demerit points to carry over to your home licence record. Ignoring it is still a gamble: the debt does not expire on its own, and it can surface when you next renew or drive there.

Tip: if a notice arrives for a trip you never took, check the plate in the photo before you pay. Misread and cloned plates do happen, and they are the cleanest dispute there is.

Common questions

Do speed camera tickets give demerit points?

In most places, no. Ontario, British Columbia, Alberta and Quebec all send camera tickets to the registered owner and add no demerit points. Most US states with speed cameras treat them as civil penalties with no points. Arizona and Michigan are the exceptions listed by the Insurance Institute for Highway Safety.

Does a speed camera ticket affect insurance?

Usually not. Insurers rate you on convictions on your driving record, and in most places a camera ticket never reaches that record. Toronto says its camera penalties do not affect your driving record, Calgary Police say camera tickets do not affect your record or insurance, and Maryland and New York City say camera violations are not used for insurance purposes.

Are speed cameras still used in Ontario?

No. Bill 56, the Building a More Competitive Economy Act, 2025, ended municipal automated speed enforcement across Ontario as of November 14, 2025. Penalties for violations before that date are still valid and must be paid or disputed. Red light cameras were not affected.

What happens if you don't pay a speed camera ticket?

Fees are added and the debt goes to collection. In Toronto that can end in licence plate denial. In British Columbia you cannot renew your driver's licence or car insurance until the ticket is paid, unless you are disputing it. In Maryland the state can refuse to register the vehicle.

Can I fight a speed camera ticket if I wasn't driving?

Usually not on that ground alone. Camera tickets are owner penalties, so the registered owner is liable whoever was driving. British Columbia removed the option to name the driver in 2018 and Quebec did the same in 2024. Stronger grounds are a misread plate, a different vehicle in the photo, or a stolen car.
