Salvage vs rebuilt title: what it means before you buy (Canada and US)

Rebuilt means inspected, not like new

A rebuilt car passed an inspection on the day it was inspected. That does not prove every repair was done well, and the brand stays on the record. Price it, insure it and inspect it as the written-off car it once was.

Drivee Editorial Updated November 15, 2026 9 min read

A salvage title means an insurer or owner declared the car a total loss. A rebuilt title means that same car was repaired and passed an inspection to go back on the road. That is the short answer to salvage vs rebuilt title in both Canada and the US. The details depend on where the car was branded, and brands do not always survive a trip across a state, provincial or national border.

What is a salvage title?

A salvage brand marks a vehicle that was written off. Usually an insurer paid out the claim as a total loss after a collision, fire, flood or theft, and decided repairs would cost too much relative to the car's value.

In the US, each state issues its own titles and sets its own rules, so the trigger for a salvage title and the exact wording on it vary from state to state. What is common is the result: a salvage vehicle generally can't be registered or driven on public roads until it is repaired and passes a state inspection. Texas, for example, says a salvage vehicle can't be registered or driven until it passes a Texas safety inspection and receives a "Rebuilt Salvage" title.

Canada has no US-style certificate of title. Each province records the brand, often called a status, on its vehicle registration record. In Ontario the brand appears on the vehicle permit, in the Used Vehicle Information Package (UVIP) and in the ministry's registration system.

Salvage vs rebuilt title: what is a rebuilt title?

A rebuilt brand goes on a salvage vehicle that has been repaired and has passed the required inspection. It tells the next buyer the car was a total loss that has since been certified for the road.

The name changes by jurisdiction. Texas issues a "Rebuilt Salvage" title. California calls it revived salvage, which its DMV defines as a salvage vehicle that was repaired and reregistered.

In Ontario, getting from Salvage to Rebuilt takes a structural inspection at a specialized DriveON Vehicle Inspection Centre. The owner has to provide photos of the car in its damaged state, documents and detailed invoices for major parts used in the rebuild. To register and plate the car you need both a Structural Inspection Certificate and a Safety Standards Certificate, and the brand changes to Rebuilt once the structural certificate is submitted at ServiceOntario.

Tip: ask the seller of a rebuilt car for that rebuild file: the damage photos, parts invoices and inspection certificate. A seller who rebuilt the car properly usually has it. One who can't produce any of it is telling you something.

What other title brands should you watch for in the US?

Salvage and rebuilt are the best known, but they are not the only brands. The California DMV lists brands commonly used across states, including:

  • Junk or nonrepairable: for parts or scrap. In Texas, a nonrepairable vehicle can't be repaired, rebuilt or made operable for public roads.
  • Water damage or flood: damaged by water. Flood cars can hide corrosion and electrical problems that show up months later.
  • Lemon law buyback or warranty returned: bought back by the manufacturer under a state lemon law.
  • Dismantled, totaled, scrap vehicle and revived junk.
  • Prior taxi, prior police and gray market: not damage, but worth knowing.

Because each state defines its own brands, a flood brand in one state does not automatically become a flood brand in the next. That gap is where title washing starts, covered below.

How do Canadian provinces brand written-off cars?

Each province runs its own program. The four largest look like this:

Quebec (SAAQ)Severely damagedRebuiltIrreparable

Ontario. The Mandatory Vehicle Branding Program has four brands: None, Salvage, Rebuilt and Irreparable. Only None and Rebuilt vehicles can be driven on Ontario roads. Brands can be assigned by the ministry, insurers, auto recyclers, salvagers, auctioneers, dealers and owners. An Irreparable vehicle can never be driven in Ontario again.

British Columbia. ICBC's statuses are normal, salvage, rebuilt, altered and non-repairable. A rebuilt status goes to a salvage vehicle after repair and inspection. A non-repairable vehicle can never be re-licensed or insured.

Alberta. A salvage vehicle can't be registered. It needs a Salvage Vehicle Inspection at a licensed Alberta inspection facility to become rebuilt, and the province says the inspection certificate must be presented at a registry within 14 days or the car needs a full inspection again. Alberta says the rebuilt status follows the vehicle for the rest of its useful life. A non-repairable vehicle can never be registered and can only be sold as scrap or for parts.

Quebec. The SAAQ gives a car that needs rebuilding a "severely damaged" status. Until it is rebuilt it can't be driven on public roads, only towed or transported. It has to pass a technical appraisal and a mechanical inspection before it gets a rebuilt status. Since January 1, 2025, rebuilders also have to show proof that driver assistance systems were recalibrated. Flooded vehicles get an irreparable status, which is final.

What happens to a brand when a car crosses a border?

Between US states. The US Department of Justice warns that brand washing can happen when a car is retitled in another state. The National Motor Vehicle Title Information System (NMVTIS) exists to close that gap. It is the only vehicle history database in the US that all states, insurers and junk and salvage yards are required by federal law to report to.

From the US into Canada. Transport Canada lists vehicles branded junk, scrap, parts-only, non-repairable, flood or water damage as inadmissible for import as a salvage vehicle meant for the road. Those can come in only for parts, and a vehicle declared parts-only at import is given a non-repairable status that the Registrar of Imported Vehicles reports to every province and territory.

Into Ontario from anywhere else. Ontario keeps a brand from another Canadian province or US state, with one twist: a car branded Rebuilt elsewhere is branded Salvage when it is registered in Ontario. It needs an Ontario structural inspection before it can be registered as Rebuilt and driven.

From Canada into the US. NMVTIS is built on US state, insurer and salvage-yard reports. A brand recorded only by a Canadian province may not show up there, so a US buyer looking at a former Canadian car should also check the provincial record or a report that covers Canadian data.

Is a rebuilt title car worth buying?

It can be, if the price reflects the brand, the repair was done properly, and you can insure and eventually sell it.

  • Insurance. Coverage on branded cars is up to each insurer. Some will ask for the inspection paperwork or limit physical damage coverage. Get a quote on the actual VIN before you buy.
  • Financing. Some lenders won't finance branded vehicles at all. If you need a loan, ask your lender first.
  • Resale. The brand stays on the record, and the next buyer will see it in the same places you did. Expect to sell at a discount too.
  • The repair itself. An inspection certificate is a minimum, not a warranty. Have your own mechanic look at frame rails, panel gaps, airbag and seatbelt warnings, signs of water in the cabin, and whether driver assistance sensors work.

How do I check if a car has a salvage or rebuilt title?

  1. Read the paperwork. In the US, the brand is printed on the title. In Ontario, check the vehicle permit and the UVIP, which a private seller is legally required to give you. Our free VIN check guide explains what the $20 UVIP covers.
  2. US records. Get an NMVTIS report through an approved provider listed at vehiclehistory.gov. It shows the current title, brand history, the latest odometer reading and total-loss reports. The National Insurance Crime Bureau's free VINCheck can flag vehicles reported as salvage or stolen by participating insurers.
  3. Provincial records. In BC, ICBC sells a vehicle claims history search. In Alberta, any registry agent sells a Vehicle Information Report showing status. In Quebec, the status appears on the registration certificate, and the ownership history you can get through SAAQclic shows it.
  4. A cross-border report. If the car has lived in more than one state or province, a history report that covers Canadian and US data is the easiest way to catch a brand that was left behind. The Drivee Car Report works for any North American VIN.
  5. An inspection. No record shows a repair that was done for cash and never reported.

Common questions

What is the difference between a salvage and a rebuilt title?

A salvage title or brand means the car was written off as a total loss and is not yet approved for the road. A rebuilt title or brand means that salvage car was repaired and passed the required inspection, so it can be registered and driven again. The rebuilt brand stays on the record.

Can you drive a car with a salvage title?

Generally no. In Ontario only vehicles branded None or Rebuilt can be driven, and Alberta will not register a salvage vehicle. US states generally require repair and inspection before a salvage vehicle can be registered. You can usually tow or transport it to a repair shop or inspection station.

Can a rebuilt title go back to a clean title?

Not legitimately in the jurisdictions covered here. The rebuilt brand records past damage and stays with the car; Alberta, for one, says it follows the vehicle for the rest of its useful life. Ontario lets an owner contest only a Salvage or Irreparable brand assigned in Ontario, within 90 days and while the car is unrepaired. A former rebuilt car showing a clean title after a move is a reason to dig deeper.

Is it worth buying a car with a rebuilt title?

It can be, if the price reflects the brand, the repair was done properly and you can insure and finance it. Get an insurance quote on the VIN, ask for the rebuild photos, invoices and inspection certificate, and have your own mechanic inspect it before you pay.

How do I find out if a car has a salvage title?

Check the title in the US or the vehicle permit and UVIP in Ontario, then confirm with an independent record: an NMVTIS report in the US, the provincial record in Canada, or a history report that covers both countries for a car that has crossed borders.

Can I import a salvage title car from the US into Canada?

Some salvage vehicles can be imported for road use, but Transport Canada lists vehicles branded junk, scrap, parts-only, non-repairable, flood or water damage as inadmissible for that. They can only come in for parts and get a permanent non-repairable status. Provinces also apply their own rules, such as Ontario's structural inspection.
